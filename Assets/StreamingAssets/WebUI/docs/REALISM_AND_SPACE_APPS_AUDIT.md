# ORBITAL model notes

ORBITAL is an independent educational game. The campaign displays simulated quantities and explicitly credited historical images.

NASA/JPL-Caltech Eyes on the Solar System supplies the static Earth surface, cloud, night and ocean-mask maps and the planetary map textures in the interactive solar atlas. The offline player bundles reprojected map images, with source URLs and SHA-256 identities in `textures/eyes/provenance.json`. The NASA Eyes application and trajectory engine are not embedded. The solar overview compresses distances and body sizes separately and uses illustrative animated angles, independently of mission navigation. Saturn's rings remain schematic. [NASA Eyes](https://eyes.nasa.gov/apps/solar-system/).

## Launch and operations

- Every campaign launch advances a numerical point-mass model with thrust, propellant consumption, staging, drag and central gravity. Flights start at 1× real time; the player can select time acceleration or skip to the calculated result. Launch guidance and scaled vehicle specifications serve the game, and the model omits structural loads and high-fidelity atmosphere and vehicle dynamics.
- GEO, legacy low lunar orbit and the guided observatory first reach a calculated Earth parking orbit. A separate, explicitly labeled handoff delivers the spacecraft to its operations environment. These contracts assess performance after placement. The GEO transfer, legacy lunar insertion and observatory halo orbit are outside those models; the parking orbit is not presented as the achieved destination orbit.
- Earth and lunar operations use two-body states and fixed 270-second resource steps. Earth uses rotating scenario stations and a nominal elevation mask. Lunar contact uses direct Earth visibility against an idealized lunar limb. SSO starts with a preset inclination and omits J2 precession. The observatory uses authored sunlight and contact fractions.
- Power is in W and energy in Wh. The simulator multiplies net power by interval hours and bounds the result by usable battery capacity. Fixed loads, incident reductions, availability and recorder limits are scaled gameplay parameters. Reports separate confirmed, stored, discarded and in-transit data in GB. Survey percentages measure effective observation time. They do not represent a mapped fraction of the real surface. Relay service uses powered active time with a modeled line of sight.

## Floodwatch and Landsat

NASA publishes a multispectral sample spacing of 30 m at 705 km altitude and a 185 km swath for the Operational Land Imager. ORBITAL uses the first two specifications to calibrate its fictional Floodwatch camera and adopts 30 m as its gameplay detail requirement. For a nadir view, the game estimates sample spacing as `30 m × mean altitude / 705 km`. At 500 km it estimates 21.3 m per pixel. At 800 km it estimates 34.0 m per pixel and the resource model withholds useful Floodwatch observations. Achieved mean altitude controls the flight result. Real retrieval depends on spectral bands, atmosphere, clouds, pointing and calibration. The estimate applies to the game's idealized near-nadir Earth orbit choices. The published swath is retained as reference and does not set coverage. [NASA OLI specifications](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/landsat/oli/)

## Transfers and arrivals

The expanded transfer contracts start from an Earth parking orbit. The engine uses circular, coplanar transfer estimates and a local impulsive braking or rendezvous check. The comet uses a perihelion scenario. Europa uses a guided Jovian flyby and an authored targeting allowance. The solar-frontier contracts use modeled escape arcs and authored crossing distances. Departure opportunities are scenario dates. The bundled JPL Horizons state vectors are a reference example. Cruise duration, remaining resources, signal time, and data receipt follow the selected educational trajectory and hardware. There is no real-time ephemeris, surface descent or continuous multi-body targeting. [NASA Trajectories](https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter4-1/) · [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/horizons/)

The Proxima sail is speculative. It assumes external acceleration to 5% of light speed, long-life power, a future optical receiver and a guided flyby. The travel and light times follow those assumptions. These are inputs to a thought experiment.

## Optional archived context investigations (Sol6.1 partial release)

Only an explicit workshop selection starts a source-backed run. Historical scenarios and reports keep their original source/scoring version. Source products, processed-bundle checksums, seed and immutable design are preserved in exports and controlled comparisons. Offline source viewers describe the observations separately from simulated follow-up, authored deadlines and scoring weights.

The lunar subset contains 23 south-polar Diviner seasonal-average bolometric brightness temperatures with nearest LOLA 240 m heights. The products span different multi-year periods. Temperatures and shadow are not compositional evidence or confirmation of ice. Target access is spherical centre-point geometry; the current investigation does not solve terrain occlusion or illumination. LOLA heights are retained as source context, rather than presented as a full terrain-lighting solution. Spacecraft sunlight remains the existing educational illumination approximation.

The Rhodes subset contains 102 archived MODIS FIRMS thermal detections from 23 July 2023, with timestamps, confidence and scan/track dimensions. Detections define inspection priorities, not burned area or exact perimeters. Confidence/100 and a 7200-second receipt-latency scale are authored priority weights, not observed fire probabilities or fire evolution. Simulated observation, recorder, transmission and receipt states remain separate.

Coordinates are retained in the source conventions. Targeting maps geographic angles onto the game's spherical Earth/Moon model; ellipsoidal corrections and detailed pointing/footprints are not solved. Source spacing is not upgraded to the fictional player's camera resolution. Empty or unsampled coverage is unknown. Floodwatch retains its original authored scenario and now offers an explicitly selected numerical flood-context subset. The full flood cloud-mask gate remains incomplete.

New launch guidance uses predicted coast apoapsis, a permitted engine restart and finite circularization. Performance is authored for the existing assets; asset resemblance is not validation of a real Ares, Atlas or Falcon vehicle. Earlier saved numerical flights retain the earlier guidance model for replay compatibility. GEO/lunar/observatory destination delivery remains the explicitly simplified handoff after a calculated Earth parking ascent.

## 11 November 2026 gameplay candidate

New runs carry `mission-continuity-1`: objective, immutable design, source identity, launch epoch convention, separate phase/mission clocks, achieved insertion, launch battery draw, upper-stage fuel and ignition state, authoritative operations/cruise state and command history. Launch electrical use assumes 40% of average bus load and no deployed arrays; this is an authored budget. Unsupported routes retain a labeled simplified handoff. Historical saves without this envelope retain their original model and outcomes.

Moon and Mars transfer plans offer `dated-conics-1`. The model uses a zero-revolution universal-variable Lambert solver, RK4 two-body spacecraft propagation and impulsive burns. Earth/Mars initial longitudes derive from the existing JPL Horizons 5 November 2026 TDB reference; subsequent body motion is circular and coplanar, not interpolated Horizons states. Lunar phase at that epoch is authored. Calendar inputs are TDB, not UTC. Constants, frame, units, range and assumptions travel with the state. Earth–Mars escape uses a scalar patched-conic energy estimate and assumes departure alignment; local escape direction/plane matching is not solved. Lunar departure uses the actual equatorial insertion transformed into ecliptic axes. Neither route is operational navigation. Other destinations retain labeled legacy approximations.

Physical departures consume actual remaining upper-stage fuel, with an explicitly authored third-start upper-engine envelope selected only for these new plans; no extra fuel is added. Too little fuel or no permitted ignition fails. A small reproducible execution error changes the true coast. Tracking observations halve an authored knowledge bound while spending one cruise interval; they do not move the spacecraft. Correction recomputes the remaining Lambert maneuver and spends spacecraft reserve. Deferring, coasting, or abandoning are separate choices. Arrival tests true endpoint error against an authored patch corridor. Capture uses actual relative arrival speed, braking fuel and a negative-energy circular local orbit; rendezvous velocity matching remains distinct in legacy families. The target-centre transfer patches to a chosen local periapsis; a resolved B-plane approach, finite burn duration and continuous multi-body transition remain outside this approximation. Failed capture can retain a shorter flyby, preserving partial evidence.

New relay runs use `two-link-traffic-1`. Authored dispatches move from source queue through spherical source visibility into recorder space, and through a separately visible Earth link to receipt. The two queues have distinct ownership, a shared catalogue link capacity, bounded storage, expiry and unique receipt credit. Urgent-first and six-interval batching policies trade delay against grouped transmissions. Power is charged through the existing payload/radio loads. This averaged-step model does not solve detailed half-duplex protocol timing. Historical relay saves keep their earlier powered-contact model.

The optional `sindh-flood-20261110-1` bundle decodes a complete official NASA LANCE Collection 6.1 F2 GeoTIFF, h24v06, composite 9–10 November 2026. Native classes are 0 no water, 1 expected surface water, 2 recurring flood, 3 unusual flood and 255 insufficient data. Thirty-two stratified native pixel centres retain classes and grid locations; 255 is always unknown. The TIFF declares a Clarke 1866 ellipsoid with unspecified datum. There is no precision datum transformation and no flood-area estimate from sparse cells. Validity and unknown masks are aligned native classifications. Cloud-specific mask entries remain unknown because this TIFF does not supply the full HDF ancillary layers; attempted historical HDF access returned HTML instead of data. Optical composites can have cloud-shadow false positives. The original Floodwatch scenario remains selectable.

New source-backed operations use `unique-target-1`: useful integration is capped at 600 seconds per archived location. Broad context uses all valid targets; priority selects flood classes, the colder lunar half or the higher-confidence fire half. These cuts and caps are authored policies. They never turn cold into ice, confidence into probability, or repeated bytes into independent geographic evidence. Source quality/missingness and simulation resource failures remain separate.

Controlled comparisons explicitly choose deployment-only or full-mission scope. Full-mission trials rerun numerical ascent with changed mass and the selected physical transfer; deployment-only trials hold the achieved placement fixed and cannot establish launch feasibility. Exports include scope, confounding flags, seed, commands and source snapshots. Trial runs do not complete campaign missions or award rewards. Reports offer optional consent-based local learning-note exports without identity fields; no participant results or mastery claims are generated.

Earth-orbit reports allow one irreversible post-mission choice. The extension spends 1 kg in a prograde impulsive burn and advances the same operations engine for 24 further 270-second steps; battery, recorder, source caps and receipt deadlines continue without resetting. Auto and conservative incident responses are the stated extension policy. A disposal option spends 3 kg in a retrograde burn, calculates the changed two-body orbit and then passivates. It may only lower the orbit; deorbit is never guaranteed. Passivation shuts down future loads without claiming zero stored energy or regulatory compliance. Outcomes append to the completed run and update the lifecycle state/clock, while the original operations summary, rewards and campaign completion remain immutable. Earlier planning-only `end-choice-1` records stay readable. The model omits drag, finite burn, reentry, debris probability and regulatory compliance. The educational trim-thruster Isp is 305 s. Lunar/deep-space reports offer passivation only until a supported continuation model exists.

The Windows host binds its HTTP disk cache to the owned package manifest SHA-256. A changed package clears only disk cache before loading assets; local saves, workshop designs and progress storage are retained. Superseded flight page-exit callbacks cannot overwrite a newer imported or newly active run. Explicit historical reimport remains available.
